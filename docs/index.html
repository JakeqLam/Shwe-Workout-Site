Easy walk or warm-up march

3–8 minutes

  • Use an easy pace that loosens the hips without increasing tailbone pain.
  • Keep steps natural; do not consciously force the pelvis level.
☁

Side-lying 360° breathing

5 slow breaths per side

  • Lie on the side with a pillow supporting the head and knees slightly bent.
  • Let the lower ribs, abdomen, back, and pelvic floor gently expand during the inhale.
  • Exhale without squeezing or performing a Kegel.
Why this version?

Side-lying avoids direct pressure on the coccyx and encourages relaxation rather than automatically strengthening a possibly overactive pelvic floor.

↗

Supported knee-to-wall ankle rocks

1–2 sets × 8 each side

  • Keep the heel down and guide the knee toward the wall over the middle toes.
  • Move only through a smooth, pain-free range.
  • Limited ankle motion can cause an earlier squat compensation.
❀

Half-kneeling hip-flexor stretch

2 × 20–30 seconds each side

  • Use padding under the knee and hold a stable support.
  • Gently tuck the belt line, then shift forward slightly.
  • Stop before the low back arches or the tailbone becomes painful.
⌒

Standing hamstring hinge stretch

2 × 20–30 seconds each side

  • Place one heel on a low step with the knee softly unlocked.
  • Hinge forward from the hips while keeping the spine long.
  • Do not round the pelvis under to chase a deeper stretch.
◇

Quadruped adductor rock-back

1–2 sets × 6–8 each side

  • From hands and knees, extend one leg gently to the side.
  • Rock the hips backward only until the inner thigh stretches.
  • Stop before the pelvis tucks or rotates.
☁
Build steady strength

Strength session

2–3 nonconsecutive days each week · begin with 2 sets

Leave 3–4 reps in reserve
▣

Goblet squat to a high target

2–3 sets × 6–10

  • Choose a target just above the depth where the visible butt wink begins.
  • Lightly touch the target; do not sit heavily onto the tailbone.
  • Use a small heel wedge only if it improves control and comfort.
Progression

Add repetitions first. Then add a small amount of weight. Lower the target only after the current depth is controlled and the next-day response is stable.

│

Supported split squat

2 sets × 6–8 each side

  • Hold a rail, rack, or sturdy counter.
  • Start with the more restricted side, then match that range and repetition count on the other side.
  • Stop if the pelvis sharply hikes, twists, or reproduces tailbone pain.
▰

Low step-up

2 sets × 6–10 each side

  • Use a low step and light hand support.
  • Drive through the whole foot without launching from the trailing leg.
  • Keep the step low enough that the pelvis stays reasonably level.
⇣

Romanian deadlift from blocks

2–3 sets × 8–10

  • Begin with the weight elevated so the starting range is comfortable.
  • Push the hips back with soft knees; stop before the pelvis tucks.
  • Use a dowel, light dumbbells, or kettlebell before heavier loading.
✧

Bird dog

2 sets × 5–8 each side · 3-second hold

  • Reach long rather than lifting the arm and leg high.
  • Keep the pelvis quiet and breathe normally.
  • Regress to moving one limb at a time if the trunk rotates.
▥

Suitcase carry

2 rounds × 20–40 seconds each side

  • Carry a manageable weight in one hand and walk slowly.
  • Stay tall without leaning away from the weight.
  • Use equal time on both sides unless a clinician specifies otherwise.
↔

Standing Pallof press

2 sets × 8–12 each side

  • Use a resistance band anchored near chest height.
  • Press forward without allowing the trunk or pelvis to rotate.
  • Choose a stance that feels stable rather than forcing both feet into a rigid position.
⇆

Lateral band walk

2 sets × 8–12 steps each direction

  • Use a light band and small, controlled steps.
  • Keep the feet mostly forward and avoid swaying side to side.
  • Stop if it increases lateral hip or tailbone pain.

Depth with control

Squat-depth rule

The goal is not an arbitrary “ass-to-grass” position. The current working depth ends just before the pelvis visibly tucks, the trunk suddenly collapses, weight shifts strongly to one side, or tailbone pain appears.

  1. Film one unloaded set from the side and one from behind.
  2. Identify the first rep depth where the pelvic tuck becomes obvious.
  3. Set the box or target slightly above that point.
  4. Train the controlled range for several sessions.
  5. Progress only when symptoms are stable the following morning.

Simple weekly schedule

  1. Monday: Mobility + strength
  2. Tuesday: Mobility + comfortable walking
  3. Wednesday: Mobility or rest
  4. Thursday: Mobility + strength
  5. Friday: Mobility + comfortable walking
  6. Weekend: Optional third light session or recovery

Avoid for now

  1. Deep loaded squats through a painful or uncontrolled tuck
  2. Heavy sitting onto a box or bench
  3. Sit-ups or seated machines that press on the coccyx
  4. Aggressive hamstring stretching with pelvic rounding

Clinical basis and useful references

This tracker combines broadly supported chronic low-back-pain exercise principles with conservative modifications intended to reduce direct coccyx pressure. The exact exercise list and dosage are a starter framework and should be individualized.

  • World Health Organization: chronic primary low-back-pain guideline
  • JOSPT/APTA clinical practice guideline: exercise, trunk activation, and movement-control training
  • Scott et al.: pelvic-floor physical therapy for chronic coccydynia
  • APTA Pelvic Health: understanding and managing coccydynia
